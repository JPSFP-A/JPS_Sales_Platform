4. New Load and Customer Acquisition — The Evidence

The residential budget assumes almost nothing from connecting new customers, and that assumption is a consequence of Hurricane Melissa, not of weak demand. Read on a July-to-July basis the customer base looks static. It is not — it lost 126,000 customers and got almost all of them back.

4.2 What is still off the grid

7,238 premises remain non-consuming against the pre-storm baseline, and restoration has stalled. The excess fell from 58,336 in January to 6,395 in June, then rose to 6,990 in July — two months without further progress. What remains is likely the hard core: premises destroyed or abandoned rather than awaiting reconnection.

4.3 What customer acquisition is actually worth

Before the hurricane, RT10 was adding 923 consuming customers a month — 8,308 over the nine clean months of 2025, while zero-consumption accounts stayed flat at around 50,800. New connections in that period were 96% consuming. Annualised, that is 11,077 consuming customers a year; at 181 kWh a month and a half-year average effect in the first year, worth approximately 12 GWh.

A year-on-year comparison across the storm gives the wrong answer here, and it is worth stating why. Measured July 2025 against July 2026, the consuming base grew by 78 customers and the zero-consumption bucket by 7,274 — which reads as though JPS is connecting customers who do not consume. It is not. The base lost 107,581 consuming customers in December 2025 and had restored all but 110 of them by July 2026, so the window nets the loss against its own repair, and the zero bucket is inflated by storm damage rather than by vacant new connections. The pre-hurricane run-rate is the correct basis for the forward view.

Growth comes from the movement between bands, not from usage inside them. The under-150 band lost 9,790 customers while the bands above it gained 9,948 — substantially the same households, moving up. Consumption per customer is flat in the middle bands and falls at both ends.

The two ends fall for different reasons. Over-950 losing 5.4% per customer is the signature of bill response and solar self-supply — the heaviest users have the most to save and are the most likely to install photovoltaic. Under-150 losing 2.5% reads as hardship or efficiency at the bottom of the book. The middle is stable.

Note that solar self-consumption appears here, not in the solar driver. A customer who installs panels and consumes their own generation without exporting never becomes a net biller; their draw simply falls inside their band. Part of the −13.4 GWh within-tier decline is therefore solar that the solar line does not capture.

Seven months to July 2026: 1,353 MWh exported and J$72.95m in credits. June and July are the two largest export months on record at roughly double the January–May rate, and July credits run at J$12,400 per net biller.

Year on year, net billers rose from 676 to 1,249 (+85%) and monthly export from 161.9 to 279.8 MWh (+73%) — an increment of about 1.4 GWh annualised. The forecast carries 4.3 GWh of residential solar erosion in FY2027, so it assumes roughly 2.9 GWh of self-consumption on top of the observable export. That is a reasonable relationship, but only the 1.4 GWh is directly measurable.

Solar is the driver most likely to be understated. Export has doubled in two months, the customer count is up 85%, and self-consumption is inferred rather than measured. FY2028 carries 8.7 GWh, roughly double FY2027, which is consistent with the trend but is an extrapolation. Every additional 500 net billers at current rates is approximately a further 1.7 GWh.

7. Storm Recovery in the Commercial Book

Hotels across all rate classes — 209 accounts — move from 193.8 GWh in FY2026 to 215.1 in FY2027 and 214.5 in FY2028. The FY2027 increase is the recovery; the flat FY2028 confirms the assumption that recovery completes rather than continuing.

Normalisation is applied to RT50 (4.66% a month), RT40 (0.28%) and RT20 (0.53%), and to no other class. RT10 carries none because its storm gap closed inside FY2026: it ran 12.5% and 15.4% below prior year in January and February, crossed above prior year in May, and was 5.0% above by July. Industrial carries none following the 29 August decision to withdraw a class-wide adjustment that was reaching non-storm-affected accounts.

8. Data Limitations

RT10 has no account-level detail. For the whole of 2026 the residential class arrives as 163 pre-aggregated rows with no account numbers, against 24,060 accounts for RT20, 2,012 for RT40, 160 for RT50, 388 for RT60-ST and 23 for RT70. RT10 is 37% of sales and the only class without account grain.

Consequences:
  • The 7,238 non-consuming premises cannot be identified, listed or assigned to a parish. No field follow-up list can be produced.
  • Parish is not held for postpaid residential — all 643,000 accounts arrive stamped "ALL". Only prepaid carries parish, and that is 17,465 customers, 2.6% of the class. The rebuild question in St. Elizabeth and Westmoreland cannot be answered from what we hold.
  • Consumption bands arrive pre-computed, so customer movement between bands is inferred from band totals rather than tracked per customer.
  • Residential variance against budget can only be reported at band level, not by customer.
This is a source extract change, not a platform change. The billing extract already retains account, parish and band per customer for RT20; applying the same treatment to RT10 would close all four gaps.

9. Basis of Preparation

  • FY2025 is billed actual. FY2026 is January–July billed actual with August–December forecast, tied to the 3,281,970 MWh submitted on 19 August 2026 through a disclosed 460.8 MWh true-up on the open months. Closed months are untouched.
  • FY2027 and FY2028 are full driver projections. Each account or cohort is projected from a trailing three-month base adjusted for macro, temperature, industry seasonality, solar, storm normalisation and confirmed customer events.
  • Operational adjustments are entered per account with a reason code and justification, and are listed in the Key Account Review.
  • System losses are held at a 27.10% twelve-month rolling rate at each December, matching FY2026. The FY2027/FY2028 targets are derived from a monthly loss path rather than set as a smooth rolling series: seasonality is taken from 2024–2025 billed actuals with the Oct–Dec 2025 hurricane months excluded, and the level solved so each year closes on target. Implied monthly rates run 22.78% to 30.72%, inside the 21.84% to 30.96% observed range. The previous targets, smoothed independently of sales seasonality, implied a 0.57% January 2028 and are superseded.
  • The engine, this report, the Executive Summary and the Sales & Generation workbook carry identical figures at rate-class level.
