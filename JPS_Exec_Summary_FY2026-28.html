What Is Driving the Outlook

  • RT70 falls 23.4% in FY2027 — the single largest movement in the forecast. Alcoa Minerals completed its transition to self-generation in August 2026 and leaves 53.8 GWh behind. MBJ Airports is assumed to offset 60% of load with a 6MW solar installation, and PAC Kingston Airport commissioned a 4MW photovoltaic system in July 2026. RT70 then flattens in FY2028 — the losses are behind it, and nothing is assumed to replace them.
  • RT50 grows 14.8% on hurricane recovery. Storm-affected load returning is the largest positive driver in the forecast. It now runs through the engine as a normalisation driver rather than as a reported overlay, so the attribution sits on the rate classes rather than on a reconciling line. It is weighted to RT50, with smaller amounts on RT40, RT20 and RT70. Caribbean Cement is excluded: it is roughly a third of RT50 but was never storm-affected, and its volume rose through 2026.
  • Residential growth is compositional, not intensive. Decomposing the July 2025 to July 2026 movement attributes 91% to customers migrating into higher consumption tiers, 6% to customer growth and only 2% to higher usage within a tier. In RT10's two largest tiers, consumption per customer fell. The 4.4% forecast for FY2027 depends on tier migration continuing at broadly the observed pace.
  • Confirmed expansion projects underpin RT40. Spanish Town Hospital's new six-storey wing, the LINCAC Cancer Centre at St. Joseph's, three National Water Commission facility expansions, Port Authority's Montego Bay berth upgrade, and retail expansion at PriceSmart, Hi-Lo and Progressive Grocers.
  • Solar self-supply is a structural headwind that roughly doubles year on year, and now appears on the commercial side as well as residential — Sagicor at three sites, Grace Food Processors and Rainforest Seafood all have photovoltaic installations booked for FY2027.

Principal Risks to the Forecast

Hurricane recovery is the largest single judgement. Recovery is assumed to complete by the end of FY2027, which is why FY2028 carries no storm uplift at all and RT50's FY2028 growth of 8.1% comes entirely from the recovered December 2027 level running for a full year rather than from any further recovery. If recovery runs slower, FY2027 is overstated and the FY2028 comparative moves with it. The measured shortfall has closed from 11.4 GWh per month in February 2026 to 6.1 GWh in July, so recovery is demonstrably under way — but independent tourism data does not yet corroborate the pace, with stopover arrivals still down 27.5% year on year in the first half of 2026.

MBJ Airports carries an unconfirmed assumption at confirmed status. The 60% offset rests on a 6MW solar plant being commissioned by end-2026, described in the supporting note as an assumption and a base-case estimate, yet recorded as a confirmed defection for eleven of twelve months. The stated output of 4.32 GWh a year is also inconsistent with a 60% offset of this customer's consumption, which would require roughly 9 GWh. The magnitude is uncertain by around 5 GWh a year.

Kingston Free Port Terminal is the largest unquantified exposure. It runs 25.3 GWh in FY2026 and is forecast to grow to 28.4 GWh, but sits on the register as a possible defection with no quantified impact. If it follows the pattern of the other port and airport accounts, FY2027 is overstated.

Concentration risk in RT50. Caribbean Cement is roughly one third of the rate class, so RT50 should be read as one large customer plus everything else rather than as a diversified class.
