Risks to the Forecast

Residential growth depends on tier migration continuing
Migration-driven growth, not new customers, is the largest single line in both bridges.
Residential (RT10+RT20) net growth is 89.2 GWh into FY2027 and 51.1 GWh into FY2028. Prior analysis this year attributed roughly 91% of residential growth to customers moving into higher consumption tiers rather than new connections or higher usage within a tier. That analysis has not been re-run against August; the direction is consistent with what August itself showed (RT10 alone beat its own forecast by 4.8 GWh in August on tier migration), but the precise split should be refreshed before this figure is presented as more than directionally sound.
Concentration in RT50 and RT70
Two classes each turn on a handful of customers.
Caribbean Cement is roughly 31% of RT50 (105.9 of 345.6 GWh in FY2026), so the class should be read as one large customer plus everything else. RT70 falls 27.6% in FY2027 on Alcoa, MBJ and PAC Kingston Airport together; the remaining industrial book is small enough that any single further loss is material to the class.
FY2026 no longer ties to the 19 August submission
The gap is 18.0 GWh and growing as more of the year closes.
This is a decision item, not a forecasting error. The submission was locked in when the year was still mostly forecast; every closed month since has come in against a live, moving base. Whether to reconcile the submission, resubmit, or hold the engine’s own number is a business decision outside this report’s scope.
