What sits behind new load

The residential budget assumes almost nothing from connecting new customers. RT10 added 9,415 connections year on year, but 7,274 of them bill zero kWh — vacant premises, service points not yet energised, prepaid meters with no purchases — and a further 1,490 went to prepaid. The non-prepaid consuming base grew by 78 customers on 583,886, worth 0.2 GWh. The entire +87.2 GWh therefore rests on existing households moving into higher consumption bands, against consumption per customer that is flat or falling inside every band. If migration slows there is no acquisition underneath it.

Risks to the Forecast

Hurricane recovery — 15.1 GWh of FY2027
If recovery stalls at the current run-rate, FY2027 growth falls from 3.8% to about 3.4%.
The forecast assumes storm-affected commercial load continues returning through FY2027 and is complete by December, which is why FY2028 carries no further recovery. The shortfall has closed from 11.4 GWh a month in February to 6.1 GWh in July, so recovery is under way. Stopover arrivals were still down 27.5% year on year in the first half, so the pace is not yet corroborated by tourism data. We will know by the March close whether the trajectory holds.
MBJ Airports — 5 GWh of FY2027, unresolved
The load is recorded as leaving, but the evidence supports a different number.
MBJ is carried as a confirmed defection for eleven months at a 60% offset, worth 14.5 GWh. The supporting note describes the underlying 6MW solar plant as an assumption rather than a commitment, and its stated output of 4.32 GWh a year is inconsistent with a 60% offset, which would require roughly 9 GWh. Either the status or the magnitude is wrong. The account manager is to confirm at the FY2027 review.
Kingston Free Port Terminal — 28.4 GWh unquantified, and unowned
Our largest single unmanaged exposure.
The account bills 25.3 GWh and is forecast to grow to 28.4. It sits on the register as a possible defection with no impact quantified and no key account manager assigned. Nothing has been deducted for it. Assigning ownership is the immediate action; quantification follows.
Residential growth depends on tier migration continuing
Migration is 91% of residential growth. If it stops, FY2027 loses roughly 77 GWh.
Decomposing the July 2025 to July 2026 movement attributes 91% of residential growth to customers moving into higher consumption tiers, 6% to customer growth and 2% to higher usage within a tier. Consumption per customer is flat or falling in RT10's two largest tiers. The forecast assumes migration continues at the observed pace; it is an observed behaviour, not a committed one, and it is the single largest assumption in the residential book.
Concentration in RT50 and RT70
Two classes each turn on a handful of customers.
Caribbean Cement is roughly one third of RT50, so the class should be read as one large customer plus everything else. RT70 falls 29.6% in FY2027 on three accounts, and after Alcoa's departure the remaining industrial book is small enough that any single further loss is material to the class.
